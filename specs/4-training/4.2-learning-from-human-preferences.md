## 4.2 Learning from human preferences

**Aim.** The reader understands how a reward model is fitted to pairwise human comparisons with the Bradley-Terry model, and what the reward model is: a learned stand-in for human judgment, with that judgment's biases.

**Text (draft).**

> People find it easier to compare two answers than to write an ideal answer or to score one on an absolute scale. RLHF therefore collects comparisons: a labeler sees two responses to a prompt and picks the better one.
>
> A reward model is a network, usually a copy of the language model with a single number as its output, trained to predict these choices. It uses the Bradley-Terry model: the probability that a labeler prefers response A to response B is σ(r(A) − r(B)), where r is the reward model's score and σ is the logistic function. Training maximizes the probability of the choices actually observed, which is again a log scoring rule (Section 2.5).
>
> The reward model is a model of human judgment, fitted to a limited sample of prompts and labelers. It inherits the labelers' tendencies, such as a preference for longer, more confident, or more flattering answers, and it can be badly wrong on responses unlike those it was trained on. When the language model is then optimized against the reward model (4.3), those errors matter.

**Formula.** For each observed preference of A over B:

```latex
P(A \succ B) = \sigma\big(r(A) - r(B)\big), \qquad \mathcal{L} = -\log \sigma\big(r(A) - r(B)\big)
```

**Image.** Two responses side by side, a labeler's tick on one, an arrow into a reward model, and the two scores it outputs.

**Interactive: "Be the labeler".**

- Display: two responses to a prompt; after labeling, the weight the fitted reward model puts on each visible feature of a response (length, correctness, confidence, flattery).
- Controls: pick the better response, about 20 times across 12 prompts; a "biased labeler" button that adds simulated labels from someone who prefers the longer answer 70% of the time; a box where the reader submits a new response for the fitted model to score.
- Try: (1) Label 20 pairs and see what the reward model concludes you value. (2) Add the biased labeler and watch the weight on length grow. (3) Score a long, confident, wrong answer.
- What to notice: the reward model learns whatever predicts the choices, including regularities nobody intended to reward.
- Build: live (logistic regression on response features); medium.

**Check.** If r(A) − r(B) = 0, what does the model predict? Answer: each response is preferred with probability 0.5.
