## 4.3 Optimizing a learned reward

**Aim.** The reader understands the KL-regularized objective used in RLHF, sees that its exact solution reweights the starting model's distribution by the reward, and sees reward-model overoptimization: as the model is pushed further, true quality first rises and then falls.

**Text (draft).**

> In RLHF the language model, now called the policy, is updated to produce responses that the reward model scores highly. If it optimized the reward alone, it would drift toward whatever outputs the reward model scores highest, including strange ones. So the objective adds a penalty for moving away from the starting model: maximize the expected reward minus β times the KL divergence from the starting model. KL divergence measures how different two probability distributions are.
>
> This objective has an exact solution. The best policy is the starting distribution reweighted by the exponential of the reward. With a large β the policy stays close to the starting model; with a small β it concentrates on the highest-reward responses. The DPO method (Rafailov et al. 2023) trains directly toward this solution, without a separate reinforcement learning step.
>
> Gao, Schulman, and Hilton (2022) measured what happens as the policy moves further from the starting model. The reward model's score keeps rising. True quality, measured by a larger "gold" reward model standing in for human judgment, rises at first and then falls. The policy has found responses that the proxy scores highly and the real standard does not. This is Goodhart's law (Section 8.1).

**Formula.** The optimal policy, where Z(x) makes the probabilities sum to 1:

```latex
\pi^{*}(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\, \exp\!\big(r(x, y)/\beta\big)
```

**Image.** Proxy reward and gold reward plotted against distance from the starting model: the proxy rises steadily, the gold reward peaks and then declines.

**Interactive: "Tilt the distribution".**

- Display: eight candidate responses to one prompt, each with a bar for its probability; the starting model's probabilities as outlines; readouts of expected proxy reward, expected true reward, and KL divergence; a plot of both expected rewards as β decreases.
- Controls: β slider (log scale); a button that reveals the true reward of each response; a switch that removes the "hack" response (very high proxy reward, low true reward, low starting probability).
- Try: (1) Lower β slowly and watch the true-reward curve. (2) Find the β that gives the highest true reward. (3) Remove the hack response and repeat.
- What to notice: optimizing hard against a proxy ends up selecting exactly the responses where the proxy and the truth disagree.
- Build: live; small.

**Check.** What policy does β → ∞ give? Answer: the starting model, unchanged.

**Links.** Back to 3.3, 4.2; forward to 8.1, 8.2. References: Ziegler et al. (2019); Gao, Schulman, and Hilton (2022); Rafailov et al. (2023).
