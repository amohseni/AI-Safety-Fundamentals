## 4.1 From pretrained model to assistant

**Aim.** The reader knows the four training stages (pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning from feedback, reinforcement learning on checkable tasks), what data and training signal each uses, and which stages are reinforcement learning.

**Text (draft).**

> A current assistant model is produced in stages.
>
> 1. **Pretraining.** The model learns to predict the next token on trillions of tokens of text and code, using the cross-entropy loss. This stage uses most of the compute and gives the model most of its knowledge. The result is a base model, which continues documents.
> 2. **Supervised fine-tuning.** The model is trained with the same loss on example conversations written or selected to show the desired behavior. It learns the format and role of an assistant.
> 3. **Reinforcement learning from human or AI feedback.** The model writes responses, a learned reward model scores them, and the model is updated to produce higher-scoring responses (4.2 and 4.3).
> 4. **Reinforcement learning on checkable tasks.** The model attempts math, coding, and other problems whose answers can be checked automatically, and is rewarded for correct answers (4.4).
>
> Stages 1 and 2 are supervised learning on fixed text. Stages 3 and 4 are reinforcement learning: the model's own outputs are scored, and it is trained to raise the score. The share of compute spent on stage 4 has grown quickly since 2024.

**Image.** A pipeline of four boxes. Each box is labeled with its data and its training signal: "fixed text, cross-entropy" for stages 1 and 2, and "the model's own outputs, scored" for stages 3 and 4.

**Interactive: "Same prompt, four checkpoints".**

- Display: one prompt and the outputs of four checkpoints of one model family; for one chosen next token, the probability each checkpoint assigns to it.
- Controls: menu of 6 prompts (a factual question, a request it should refuse, a question with a false premise, a math problem, an ambiguous instruction, a request for an opinion).
- Data: real outputs from an open model family that publishes every stage. AI2's OLMo and Tülu releases include base, fine-tuned, preference-trained, and RL-trained checkpoints.
- Try: (1) Compare how each checkpoint handles the false premise. (2) Find where refusals first appear. (3) Compare the base and final models' probabilities on a multiple-choice answer.
- What to notice: each stage changes behavior a lot and knowledge little.
- Build: precomputed; small.

**Check.** Which stage gives the model most of its knowledge of the world? Answer: pretraining.
