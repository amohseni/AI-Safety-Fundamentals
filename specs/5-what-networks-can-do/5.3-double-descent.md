## 5.3 Double descent

**Aim.** The reader sees that as model size grows, test error can fall, rise to a peak at the size where the model can just fit every training example, and then fall again; and understands why the peak occurs there.

**Text (draft).**

> Section 2.8 showed the classical U-shaped curve. If the model keeps growing past the size at which it can fit every training example exactly, called the interpolation threshold, test error often falls again, sometimes below the bottom of the U. This is double descent (Belkin et al. 2019; Nakkiran et al. 2019).
>
> The peak occurs at the threshold because a model with just enough capacity to fit the data has essentially one way to do it, and that one fit must bend sharply to pass through every noisy point. A model with many more parameters than data points has many exact fits, and gradient descent started from small weights tends to find a smooth one; in linear models it finds the fit with the smallest weights (the minimum-norm solution).
>
> Double descent also appears as training time grows and as the dataset grows, and it is strongest when the labels are noisy. It is one reason the classical advice to keep models small does not carry over to large networks.

**Check.** Why does regularization remove the peak? Answer: it stops the fit from passing exactly through every noisy point at the threshold.
