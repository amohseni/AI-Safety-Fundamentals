## 5.6 Emergence

**Aim.** The reader knows the claim that some abilities appear suddenly as models grow, the counterargument that much of the suddenness comes from all-or-nothing metrics, and what remains unsettled.

**Text (draft).**

> Wei et al. (2022) reported "emergent abilities": tasks on which performance stayed near chance as models grew and then jumped above chance at some size. If abilities appear suddenly, scaling laws for the loss would not warn us of them, which matters for safety.
>
> Schaeffer, Miranda, and Koyejo (2023) argued that much of this suddenness is produced by the way performance is measured. Exact-match accuracy on a five-digit answer gives no credit until every digit is right. If accuracy per digit improves smoothly, the chance of getting all five digits right is that accuracy raised to the fifth power, which stays near zero and then rises steeply. Measured with a continuous metric, the same models often improve smoothly.
>
> The debate is not fully settled. Some sharp transitions remain under continuous metrics, and in practice what matters is often whether a task gets done, which is an all-or-nothing outcome. The practical lesson for forecasting is to track the continuous measures that underlie the outcomes we care about.

**Image.** The same models plotted two ways: exact-match accuracy against model size (a sudden jump) and per-digit accuracy against model size (a smooth curve).
