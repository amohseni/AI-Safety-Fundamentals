## 5.2 Learning as selection

**Aim.** The reader sees that simple reinforcement learning rules follow, in the limit, the replicator equation from evolutionary game theory, and takes away a general way of seeing: training by reward selects whatever behavior was rewarded, whether or not anyone designed that behavior.

**Text (draft).**

> In Cross's (1973) reinforcement rule, an agent chooses among actions with probabilities xᵢ. After choosing action i and receiving a payoff between 0 and 1, it shifts probability toward i in proportion to the payoff. Börgers and Sarin (1997) proved that as the step size goes to zero, under stated conditions, the learning process follows the replicator equation from evolutionary biology: the probability of an action grows at a rate proportional to how much its payoff exceeds the current average payoff.
>
> The same equation appears elsewhere in learning. Exponential-weights learning gives exactly the replicator equation in continuous time, and policy-gradient learning with a softmax policy on a simple choice problem gives a closely related flow.
>
> These results concern simple learning rules, not the training of language models. Their value here is as a way of seeing: learning from reward is a selection process. Behaviors that earned more reward than average become more common, whether or not anyone intended them. Sections 8.2 and 8.4 apply this way of seeing to reward hacking and instrumental convergence.

**Formula.** The replicator equation, where π̄ is the average payoff:

```latex
\dot{x}_i = x_i\,(\pi_i - \bar{\pi}), \qquad \bar{\pi} = \sum_j x_j \pi_j
```

**Image.** A triangle (the simplex of three actions) with the replicator flow drawn as arrows and one noisy learning path laid over it.

**Interactive: "Learning and selection on a simplex".**

- Display: the triangle of action probabilities, the replicator flow field, and the paths of several learning runs.
- Controls: payoffs of the three actions (or a 3 by 3 game against a fixed opponent, such as rock-paper-scissors); learning rule (Cross, exponential weights, softmax policy gradient); step size; number of runs.
- Try: (1) With step size 0.1 and then 0.001, compare the learning paths to the replicator curve. (2) Make two actions nearly equal in payoff and watch selection slow down. (3) Switch to rock-paper-scissors.
- What to notice: as the step size shrinks, the learner's path follows the selection dynamics.

**Check.** In the replicator equation, what happens to an action whose payoff equals the average? Answer: its probability is momentarily unchanged.
