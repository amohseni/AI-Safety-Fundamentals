## 5.2 Learning as selection

**Aim.** The reader sees that simple reinforcement learning rules follow, in the limit, the replicator equation from evolutionary game theory, and takes away a general way of seeing: training by reward selects whatever behavior was rewarded, whether or not anyone designed that behavior.

**Text (draft).**

> In Cross's (1973) reinforcement rule, an agent chooses among actions with probabilities xᵢ. After choosing action i and receiving a payoff between 0 and 1, it shifts probability toward i in proportion to the payoff. Börgers and Sarin (1997) proved that as the step size goes to zero, under stated conditions, the learning process follows the replicator equation from evolutionary biology: the probability of an action grows at a rate proportional to how much its payoff exceeds the current average payoff.
>
> The same equation appears elsewhere in learning. Exponential-weights learning gives exactly the replicator equation in continuous time, and policy-gradient learning with a softmax policy on a simple choice problem gives a closely related flow.
>
> These results concern simple learning rules, not the training of language models. Their value here is as a way of seeing: learning from reward is a selection process. Behaviors that earned more reward than average become more common, whether or not anyone intended them. Sections 8.2 and 8.4 apply this way of seeing to reward hacking and instrumental convergence.

**Formula.** The replicator equation, where π̄ is the average payoff:

```latex
\dot{x}_i = x_i\,(\pi_i - \bar{\pi}), \qquad \bar{\pi} = \sum_j x_j \pi_j
```

**Check.** In the replicator equation, what happens to an action whose payoff equals the average? Answer: its probability is momentarily unchanged.

**Changes made while building (2026-10-16).**

- Rock-paper-scissors is played against opponents who choose as the learner does (a learner against copies of itself, payoffs win 1, tie 0.5, loss 0), since against a fixed opponent the payoffs would just be three constants.
- Exponential weights is the full-information version: every action's score grows by the payoff it would have earned. With fixed payoffs it lies exactly on the replicator curve at every step size; an importance-weighted bandit version was tried and dropped because its updates explode when a probability is tiny.
- Börgers and Sarin's result is stated with its conditions: payoffs between 0 and 1, step size to zero with steps per unit time growing, any bounded stretch of time, probability approaching 1; the sidenote notes that long-run behavior can differ (task 4 shows drift in rock-paper-scissors).
- The softmax policy gradient flow is derived on the page: dtheta/dt = x_i (pi_i - pibar), so dx_i/dt = x_i (g_i - sum_j x_j g_j) with g_i = x_i(pi_i - pibar), the replicator equation with each advantage multiplied again by x_i; same rest points, slower selection of rare good actions. Tested (`tools/test-page-5-2.mjs`): from (0.05, 0.15, 0.8) with payoffs 0.9, 0.6, 0.3 the PG curve departs from the replicator curve by up to 0.31.
- Distances are measured as how far a path strays from a curve's shape (ignoring speed), averaged over 10 seeded runs; the same function (`runAndMeasure`) produces the tool's readout and the test numbers.
- A fourth task (rock-paper-scissors) was added; the time horizon is 60 for fixed payoffs and 120 for rock-paper-scissors. A second check tests the selection view directly.
- The simplex plot is a new shared component, `docs/js/lib/s45-simplex.js`, usable by 8.5.
