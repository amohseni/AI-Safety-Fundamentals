## 2.2 The neuron

**Aim.** The reader can compute a neuron's output by hand and sees that a single neuron divides its input space with a hyperplane.

**Text (draft).**

> An artificial neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs, adds a constant called the bias, and passes the result through a fixed function called the activation function. In symbols, output = σ(w · x + b). The weights w say how much each input counts, and the bias b shifts the threshold.
>
> The inputs where w · x + b = 0 form a line in two dimensions and a hyperplane in more. The neuron's output depends only on which side of that hyperplane an input falls and how far from it. So a single neuron, whatever its activation function, can separate two classes only with a hyperplane. This is the perceptron's limit from Section 1.2.

**Image.** A neuron with three inputs x1, x2, x3, a weight on each arrow, a summing node with the bias, an activation curve, and the output.

**Interactive: "Perceptron playground", neuron mode** (shared with 1.2).

- Display: points in the plane; the neuron's output shown as background shading; the weight vector drawn as an arrow; the line w · x + b = 0.
- Controls: drag the tip of the weight arrow; bias slider; activation function (step, sigmoid, ReLU).
- Try: (1) Make the arrow longer without changing its direction and watch the shading. (2) Rotate the arrow and watch the line. (3) Find weights that separate the two classes. (4) Try XOR again.
- What to notice: the direction of w sets the line's orientation (the line is perpendicular to w), the length of w sets how sharp the transition is, and the bias sets the line's position.
- Build: live; small (shared component).

**Check.** What happens to the neuron's line if you double both w and b? Answer: the line stays where it is; the transition across it becomes sharper.

**Links.** Back to 1.2; forward to 2.3.

**Changes made while building (2026-10-16).**

- The weight arrow is drawn from the origin at one quarter of its length, so lengths up to 7 fit the picture; the page says so.
- Task 1 is corrected. With the bias held fixed, making the arrow longer sharpens the transition and also moves the line toward the origin, because the line's distance from the origin is |b|/|w|. The spec's "what to notice" (length sets sharpness, bias sets position) holds only if b is scaled with w; the page says that the bias measured against the length of w sets the position, and the check question (double w and b) tests exactly this.

**Review (2026-10-16).**

- Accuracy: "a single neuron, whatever its activation function, can separate two classes only with a hyperplane" is false for activation functions that rise and fall (a neuron with |z| or a bump function can compute XOR; GELU dips below zero). The text and takeaway now state the claim for activation functions that never decrease, as step, sigmoid, and ReLU do, and say why (any threshold of the output is a half-plane).
- The theorem now states its hypothesis (w not zero), and "multiplying w and b by the same positive number sharpens the transition" became "by the same number greater than 1".
- "The darker, the larger" and "the black line" were wrong in the dark theme; now "the heavier the shading" and "the solid line".
- On phones the neuron diagram was drawn at 300 pixels and scaled to 284, shrinking its labels; it is now drawn at the available width, and the "output" label moved below its arrow so it does not touch the activation curve.
