## 2.4 Layers bend space

**Aim.** The reader sees a multilayer network as a sequence of geometric transformations that move the data until one hyperplane separates the classes.

**Text (draft).**

> Each layer applies a linear map, which rotates, stretches, and shears space, and then an activation function, which bends or folds it. ReLU, for example, flattens every negative coordinate to zero.
>
> Two interleaved spirals cannot be separated by one line in the plane. A network that classifies them moves the points through its hidden layers until, in the coordinates of the last hidden layer, a single hyperplane separates the two classes. The final layer is just a linear classifier applied to that transformed space.
>
> This picture comes from Chris Olah's 2014 essay "Neural Networks, Manifolds, and Topology" and from 3Blue1Brown's video series on neural networks. It is a good way to think about what hidden layers do: they compute a new representation of the input in which the task is easy.

**Image.** Four panels of the spirals dataset with a grid drawn over the plane: the input space, after hidden layer 1, after hidden layer 2, and the last space with the separating line.

**Interactive: "Watch the plane bend".**

- Display: the data and a grid, transformed step by step through the network. Width 2 is drawn in the plane; width 3 is drawn in a rotatable 3D view.
- Controls: dataset (circles, XOR, spirals); hidden width (2 or 3); train and pause; a slider that moves the grid and points smoothly through each step, with the linear map and the activation shown as separate steps.
- Try: (1) On circles, find the layer at which the classes become separable by a line. (2) On spirals with width 2, watch training fail; switch to width 3 and watch it succeed. (3) Pause mid-training and step through the layers.
- What to notice: the last layer's job is easy; the hidden layers do the work of making it easy.
- Build: live, with an animated grid; large. One of the first eight tools to build.

**Check.** In this picture, what does the final layer do? Answer: it draws one hyperplane in the space produced by the last hidden layer.
