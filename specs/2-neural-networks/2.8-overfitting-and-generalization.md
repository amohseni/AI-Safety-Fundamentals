## 2.8 Overfitting and generalization

**Aim.** The reader can tell training error from test error, sees the classical U-shaped relation between model size and test error, and learns that large networks do not follow it (setting up 5.3).

**Text (draft).**

> The goal of training is low error on new examples. Error on held-out examples not used in training is called test error. A model with too few parameters cannot fit the pattern in the data; this is underfitting. In the classical picture, a model with too many parameters fits the noise in the training set as well as the pattern; this is overfitting, and test error rises. Classical statistics therefore recommends a model size in between.
>
> Large neural networks have many more parameters than training examples. They can fit randomly relabeled data perfectly (Zhang et al. 2017), and yet they generalize well on real data. That combination contradicts the classical picture. Section 5.3 shows what happens instead. Regularization methods, such as weight decay, dropout, and stopping training early, reduce overfitting in both pictures.
