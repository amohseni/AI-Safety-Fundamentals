## 9.1 Open problems and how to start

**Aim.** The reader leaves with a map of open problems, each tied to the pages that prepare for it, and a first project for each.

**Text (draft).**

> Each problem below is open, matters for safety, and can be started with the tools from this site and a single GPU or less. The table lists the pages that prepare you for each problem, a first project small enough for a semester, and a paper to start from.

| Problem | Why it matters | Pages | A first project | Start with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Are most features linear? | Most interpretability tools assume they are | 6.2, 6.4 | Search a small model for a feature that is represented nonlinearly | Engels et al. (2024) |
| Is written reasoning faithful? | Monitoring reasoning models depends on it | 4.4, 8.6 | Replicate an early-answering test on an open reasoning model | Lanham et al. (2023); Korbak et al. (2025) |
| Probes that generalize | Lie detection and monitoring need probes that work on new kinds of input | 6.3, 7.2 | Train truth probes and test them across negations and character-opinion prompts | Levinstein and Herrmann (2025); Herrmann and Levinstein (2026), Ch. 4 |
| Reward model overoptimization | Every model trained with RLHF is optimized against a proxy | 4.3, 8.1 | Reproduce a best-of-n overoptimization curve with small open models | Gao, Schulman, and Hilton (2022) |
| Goal attribution from internals | Telling misaligned goals apart from mistakes | 7.1, 7.3 | Find a goal representation in a small RL agent and test it by intervention | Herrmann and Levinstein (2026), Ch. 5 |
| Instrumental convergence in trained agents | The theorems cover optimal policies only | 5.2, 8.4 | Test whether trained, not optimal, agents on graph environments seek power | Turner et al. (2021) |
| Valid capability evaluations | Deployment decisions rest on them | 5.6, 8.6 | Build a small evaluation for one capability and test whether it measures what it claims | Kwa et al. (2025) |

> Places to start: the ARENA curriculum (hands-on exercises for most of Sections 3 and 6), the TransformerLens library, the AI Alignment Forum, and mentorship programs such as SPAR and MATS.
