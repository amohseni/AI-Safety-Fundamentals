## 0.1 Start here

**Aim.** The reader knows what the site will let them do, the order of its sections, how a page works, and which examples run through it.

**Text (draft).**

> This site explains how modern AI systems work, in enough depth to follow current AI safety research and to start contributing to it. It assumes linear algebra, calculus, and probability, and no machine learning.
>
> The site has nine sections. Section 1 is a short history. Sections 2 and 3 explain what a neural network is and how a transformer, the architecture behind current language models, computes its output. Section 4 explains how today's models are trained. Section 5 asks what these systems can do in principle and collects results that surprised the field. Sections 6 and 7 cover how researchers try to read what a trained network has learned, and when it is correct to say a system believes or wants something. Section 8 explains why a trained system can pursue goals its designers did not intend. Section 9 lists open problems and ways to start on them.
>
> Every page answers one question with one interactive tool. Read the short text, do the tasks under the tool, and then read what to notice. Each page ends with one check question.
>
> A few objects recur. A small network that labels points in the plane appears in Section 2. A small transformer that learns to add numbers modulo 113 appears in Section 5, and in Section 6 you open it up and read its algorithm. GPT-2 small, a real language model, appears wherever we need one.

**Image.** The site map: nine section boxes from left to right, with the running examples drawn as colored threads passing through the pages where they appear.

**Interactive: "Site map".**

- Display: a clickable map of all 50 pages. Nodes are pages, grouped by section; edges mean "uses an idea from".
- Controls: hover or tap a node to see the page's question and takeaway; click to open it. Pages the reader has visited are filled in (stored in the browser).
- Try: (1) Find the shortest path of pages from 2.5 (proper scoring rules) to 8.1 (Goodhart's law). (2) Find every page that uses GPT-2 small.
- What to notice: Sections 6 to 8 depend on a small number of earlier pages, so a reader in a hurry can take a short path.
- Build: live, small.

**Check.** None; this page is orientation.

**Links.** Every section's first page.
