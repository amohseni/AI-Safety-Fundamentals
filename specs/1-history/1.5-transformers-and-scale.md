## 1.5 Transformers and scale (2017 to 2022)

**Aim.** The reader knows that the transformer replaced recurrence with attention, and that from 2018 to 2022 progress came mainly from training larger transformers on more text, guided by empirical scaling laws.

**Text (draft).**

> Before 2017, language models read text one word at a time using recurrent networks such as the LSTM. Attention, introduced for translation in 2014, let a model look directly at any earlier word. The 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need" dropped the recurrence and built the whole model from attention and simple feed-forward layers. The resulting architecture, the transformer, processes all positions of a text in parallel during training, which suits GPUs.
>
> OpenAI's GPT models are transformers trained to predict the next token of internet text. GPT-1 (2018) had about 117 million parameters, GPT-2 (2019) 1.5 billion, and GPT-3 (2020) 175 billion. GPT-3 could do many tasks from a few examples given in the prompt, without further training.
>
> Kaplan et al. (2020) found that a language model's loss falls as a power law in its number of parameters, its amount of training data, and its training compute, over many orders of magnitude. Hoffmann et al. (2022), the Chinchilla paper, revised the recommended balance: for a fixed compute budget, train a smaller model on more data, about 20 tokens per parameter (Section 3.8). Sutton's essay "The Bitter Lesson" (2019) states the view behind this period: general methods that make use of more computation have beaten methods built on human knowledge.
