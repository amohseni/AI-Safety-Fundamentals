## 3.7 A full forward pass

**Aim.** The reader can trace a prompt through the whole model, naming every operation in order, and knows where the parameters are.

**Text (draft).**

> Here is everything GPT-2 small does to produce one next-token distribution.
>
> 1. Split the text into tokens (3.1).
> 2. Look up each token's embedding and add a position embedding (3.2).
> 3. Repeat 12 times: normalize, apply attention with 12 heads, and add the result to the residual stream; normalize, apply the MLP, and add the result to the residual stream (3.4 to 3.6).
> 4. Normalize, multiply by the unembedding matrix to get one score per vocabulary token, and apply softmax (3.3).
>
> Apart from tokenization and the lookups, every step is a matrix multiplication except softmax, the MLP's activation function, and normalization. Of GPT-2 small's 124 million parameters, about 39 million are in the token embedding matrix. Each layer has about 2.4 million attention parameters and 4.7 million MLP parameters, so the MLPs hold about two thirds of the per-layer weights.
>
> Current models change the details: rotary position encodings, RMSNorm, SwiGLU activations, more layers and heads, and sometimes mixture-of-experts MLPs, in which each token uses only some of the MLP weights. The overall structure is the same.

**Image.** The full pipeline as a horizontal diagram, with the tensor shape on each arrow (\[n tokens\], \[n × 768\], ..., \[n × 50,257\]).

**Interactive: "Step through GPT-2".**

- Display: the pipeline diagram with the current step highlighted; the current tensor as a heat map with its shape; the parameter count of the current operation; a running chart of where the parameters are.
- Controls: prompt menu (5 prompts); Next and Back; jump to any step.
- Try: (1) Step through once from tokens to probabilities. (2) Find the step where the shape changes from n × 768 to n × 50,257. (3) Find the largest single matrix in the model.
- What to notice: the whole model is a short list of simple operations, repeated.
- Build: precomputed from GPT-2 small; medium.

**Check.** Which operations in a transformer are not linear? Answer: softmax (in attention and at the output), the MLP's activation function, and normalization.

**Links.** Back to 3.1 to 3.6. References: Radford et al. (2019), GPT-2; Karpathy, "nanoGPT" and "Let's build GPT" (2023).

**Changes made while building (2026-10-16).**

- Built 2026-11-06 from GPT-2 small (revision 607a30d), `tools/3-7/`. The forward pass is recomputed by hand from the model's modules and checked against the model's output. Every intermediate tensor of all 12 layers is stored for 5 prompts (2.3 MB), at 15 levels per tensor for display; the scores over 50,257 tokens are stored as the largest value in each bin of 64 ids, with the top 10 exact.
- The tool has 127 steps: tokens, two lookups, the sum; ten steps in each layer (normalize; queries, keys, values; attention weights; weighted average and output matrix; add; normalize; MLP first matrix; GELU; MLP second matrix; add); final normalize, unembedding, softmax. A layer slider jumps between layers.
- The spec text said "every step is a matrix multiplication except softmax, the activation function, and normalization". Two multiplications inside attention (queries by keys, weights by values) multiply two quantities computed from the input, so they are not linear either, and most matrix steps add a bias. The text and the check now say so.
- The unembedding in GPT-2 is the token embedding matrix, transposed (tied); the page says so and counts it once. Parameter counts are read from the model: 124,439,808 total, 38,597,376 token embedding, 2,362,368 attention and 4,722,432 MLP per layer.
- Tasks: the first step wider than 768 (queries, keys, values, 2,304, not the MLP); the largest matrix; the MLP's share of a layer; how many positions get a distribution. The parameter counts and the nonlinearity paragraph moved below the tasks. The step list shows names only; shapes appear for the current step, so task 1 is not answered at a glance.
- Checks: which step can mix positions; what normalization does to a doubled input; which steps are not linear (the spec's question, with the attention products included).
- The static figure is a line of operations with the shape after each, placed after the tasks. A sentence on current models (rotary positions, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, mixture of experts) cites the original papers and LLaMA.
