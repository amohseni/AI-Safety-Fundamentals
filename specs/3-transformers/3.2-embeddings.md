## 3.2 Embeddings

**Aim.** The reader understands that each token becomes a learned vector, that geometry in the vector space carries meaning (similar words lie close together; some relations are directions), and knows the caveats on the well-known analogy results.

**Text (draft).**

> The first layer of a language model is a lookup table. Each token id is mapped to a learned vector, called its embedding; GPT-2 small uses 768 numbers per token. Embeddings are learned along with the rest of the model, so tokens that occur in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.
>
> Some relations between words show up as directions. In word2vec (2013), the vector for "king" minus "man" plus "woman" lands near "queen". This is an early example of the linear representation hypothesis (Section 6.2): the hypothesis that concepts correspond to directions in a model's vector space.
>
> The analogy results are weaker than they are often presented. The standard test excludes the three input words from the possible answers. Without that exclusion, the nearest vector to king − man + woman is often "king" itself (Nissim et al. 2020). The analogy illustrates a real but noisy tendency.

**Image.** A 2D projection of embeddings for six countries and their capitals, with roughly parallel arrows from each country to its capital.

**Interactive: "Embedding space".**

- Display: a 2D or 3D projection of about 2,000 word vectors; a list of nearest neighbors by cosine similarity.
- Controls: projection method (PCA or UMAP); word search; analogy calculator a − b + c showing the top 5 results, with a switch for excluding the input words.
- Try: (1) Find the neighbors of "bank". (2) Run king − man + woman with the exclusion switched off, then on. (3) Try three country-to-capital analogies.
- What to notice: nearest neighbors are informative; analogy arithmetic works only some of the time and depends on the exclusion rule.
- Build: precomputed vectors (a word2vec or GloVe subset); medium.

**Check.** Where do embedding vectors come from? Answer: they are parameters, learned by gradient descent with the rest of the model.

**Links.** Forward to 6.2. References: Mikolov et al. (2013); Nissim, van Noord, and van der Goot (2020), "Fair is better than sensational".

**Changes made while building (2026-10-16).**

- Built 2026-11-06 under the brief's addendum. The tool uses the GoogleNews word2vec vectors (Mikolov et al. 2013): the 4,000 most frequent entries that are a plain lowercase or capitalized word, plus 39 words the tasks and figure need (4,039 in all, 300 numbers each, stored as bytes, 2.0 MB). Neighbors and analogies are computed live over these words; the key results match those over the first 300,000 entries of the full vocabulary. Data script and download instructions in `tools/3-2/`.
- The PCA and UMAP maps are computed in advance (UMAP by umap-learn, seed 0). A readout counts how many of a word's ten nearest neighbors are among the ten nearest dots on the map, and task 4 uses it (king: PCA 0, UMAP 4; over 577 words, PCA 0.3 and UMAP 3.3 on average).
- Nissim et al. (2020) do not report the result for king − man + woman itself; they report that on the standard test set accuracy falls from 74% to 21% when the input words are allowed, mostly because an input is returned. The page measures king − man + woman directly on the shipped vectors (king 0.799, queen 0.712) and cites Nissim et al. for the aggregate numbers.
- The caveat paragraph, the linear representation hypothesis statement, and the figure moved below the tasks, so task 2 is not answered in advance.
- Spec task 3 ("try three country-to-capital analogies") now names three: Tokyo − Japan + France (Paris), Paris − France + Italy (Milan first, Rome second), Oslo − Norway + Austria (Vienna; Austria with the exclusion off). Task 1's answer: bank's ten neighbors are all financial.
- The figure uses France, Germany, Poland, Russia, Egypt, and Japan; the PCA of their twelve vectors (computed in the browser) gives arrows whose directions differ by at most 13 degrees. A set with Italy and Spain put the European labels on top of each other.
- The check "where do embedding vectors come from" would be answered by recalling the text; it became "what would the neighbors of bank be before training" (random). A second check asks which vector word2vec uses for bank in a river sentence.
- Added a measured statement that GPT-2 small's own token embeddings behave the same way (neighbors of " bank"; " king" first and " queen" second for the analogy), checked by `tools/3-2/gpt2_check.py`.
- Added a sidenote reference for UMAP (McInnes, Healy, and Melville 2018) and Mikolov, Yih, and Zweig (2013) for the king/queen example.
