## 3.4 Attention

**Aim.** The reader understands attention as each position taking a weighted average of information from earlier positions, with weights from query-key dot products passed through softmax, and can compute a small example by hand.

**Text (draft).**

> Attention is the only place in a transformer where information moves between positions. Every other operation acts on each position separately.
>
> At each position, the model computes three vectors from the current representation: a query (what this position is looking for), a key (what this position offers to positions that look at it), and a value (what this position passes along if attended to). For position i, the model takes the dot product of i's query with the key of every position j up to and including i. Softmax turns these scores into weights that add up to 1, and position i receives the weighted average of the values.
>
> In a language model, a position may attend only to itself and earlier positions, so the model cannot look at the token it is trying to predict. The scores are divided by the square root of the vector length, to keep softmax from becoming too sharp. The query, key, and value vectors come from three learned matrices, so what each position looks for and offers is itself learned.

**Formula.** M sets the scores for future positions to minus infinity:

```latex
\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right) V
```

**Image.** The sentence "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired" with lines from "it" to the earlier tokens, line thickness proportional to attention weight, the thickest going to "animal".

**Interactive: "Attention by hand and in GPT-2".** One of the first eight tools to build.

- Tab 1, by hand: four tokens, each with a 2D query and key drawn as arrows the reader can drag. Live display of the score matrix, the softmax weights, and the output vectors. Switches for the causal mask and the scaling.
- Tab 2, real model: choose a sentence and a layer and head of GPT-2 small; tap a token to see its attention to earlier tokens as lines and as a heat map.
- Try: (1) In tab 1, make token 4 attend almost entirely to token 2. (2) Make two keys identical and watch the weights split between them. (3) In tab 2, find a head in which every token attends to the token just before it.
- What to notice: a weight is high when a query and a key point the same way; the output is a mix of values.
- Build: tab 1 live, tab 2 precomputed; medium.

**Check.** If all keys are identical, what does attention output? Answer: the plain average of the values over the allowed positions.

**Links.** Forward to 3.5, 3.6, 6.7. References: Bahdanau, Cho, and Bengio (2014); Vaswani et al. (2017); 3Blue1Brown, "Attention in transformers, visually explained".
