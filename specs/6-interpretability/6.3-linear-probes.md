## 6.3 Linear probes

**Aim.** The reader can train a linear probe, interpret its accuracy correctly, and apply the two checks (control tasks and causal intervention) that separate "the information can be read out" from "the model uses it".

**Text (draft).**

> A linear probe is a logistic regression trained to predict some property, such as a word's part of speech or the truth of a statement, from a model's activations at one layer (Alain and Bengio 2016). High accuracy shows that the property can be read out linearly at that layer.
>
> Being readable is weaker than being used. A probe can succeed by picking up information the model carries but never uses, and a flexible probe can learn the task by itself. Hewitt and Liang (2019) proposed control tasks: train the same probe to predict random labels assigned to each word type. If the probe also fits the control task well, its success on the real task says little about the model. The stronger check is causal: remove or change the probed direction and see whether the model's behavior changes (6.6, 6.7).
>
> Probes are also used to monitor models. A probe trained to detect a dangerous property, such as the model writing a deceptive answer, can run on every output. For such uses the key question is whether the probe still works on cases unlike its training data. Section 7.2 discusses probes for belief and lying.

**Image.** Probe accuracy by layer for three properties in GPT-2 small, each with a dashed control-task curve beneath it.

**Interactive: "Train a probe".**

- Display: train and test accuracy; accuracy by layer; control-task accuracy alongside; the difference between the two (selectivity); after ablation, the change in the model's loss on the relevant tokens.
- Controls: property (part of speech, sentiment, whether a token is inside quotation marks); layer; probe type (linear, or a small two-layer network); regularization; an "ablate" button that removes the probe's direction from the activations.
- Data: GPT-2 small activations for all 12 layers, computed in advance; probes trained live in the browser.
- Try: (1) Find the layer where sentiment is most readable. (2) Switch to the two-layer probe and compare its control-task accuracy. (3) Ablate the direction: does the model's behavior change?
- What to notice: accuracy alone overstates what a probe shows; selectivity and ablation say more.
- Build: medium.

**Check.** A probe scores 95% on the real task and 93% on the control task. What does that suggest? Answer: the probe is mostly learning the task itself; it is weak evidence that the representation encodes the property in a readable form.
