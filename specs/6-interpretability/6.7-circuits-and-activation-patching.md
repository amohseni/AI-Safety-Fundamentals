## 6.7 Circuits and activation patching

**Aim.** The reader understands circuit analysis (finding the components and connections that carry out a behavior), uses activation patching as its causal test, and sees one complete example: the modular-addition transformer.

**Text (draft).**

> A circuit is a set of model components, such as particular attention heads and MLP neurons, together with the connections between them, that carries out a specific behavior. Finding one is the most detailed form of mechanistic interpretability.
>
> The main tool is activation patching. Run the model on a clean input and on a corrupted input that differs in one relevant way: for example, "When Mary and John went to the store, John gave a drink to" and the same sentence with a different name in the second clause. Then run the corrupted input again, but copy in one component's activation from the clean run. If the output moves back toward the clean answer ("Mary"), that component carries the information that decides the answer. Wang et al. (2022) used patching to map a circuit of 26 attention heads in GPT-2 small that performs this task.
>
> The modular-addition transformer from 5.4 has been reverse-engineered completely (Nanda et al. 2023). Its embedding maps each number a to sines and cosines of a few frequencies w. The attention and MLP layers combine a and b using the identity cos(w(a + b)) = cos(wa)cos(wb) − sin(wa)sin(wb). The output layer picks the c that makes cos(w(a + b − c)) largest, which happens when c = (a + b) mod 113. Each step can be checked by reading the weights and by patching.
>
> Circuit analysis of large models is slow and incomplete. Attribution graphs (Lindsey et al. 2025) automate part of it by tracing features through a simplified replacement model. With them, researchers found that Claude 3.5 Haiku, writing a rhyming couplet, chose the rhyme word before writing the line that ends with it.

**Image.** The modular-addition algorithm as a diagram: a and b go to points on circles at frequency w; the trigonometric identity combines them; the readout compares the result with each candidate c; the answer is c = (a + b) mod 113.

**Interactive: "Patch it".**

- Tab 1, GPT-2 small on the name task: a 12 by 12 grid of heads. Tap a head to patch its clean activation into the corrupted run and see the change in the logit difference (the score for the correct name minus the score for the wrong name). After a few tries, the full patching heat map is revealed.
- Tab 2, the modular-addition model: the frequency spectrum of the embedding (a few spikes); pick a frequency to see the numbers 0 to 112 laid out on a circle; ablate frequencies one at a time and watch test accuracy.
- Try: (1) Find the three heads whose patching restores the most logit difference. (2) In tab 2, find the key frequencies and ablate all the others. (3) Ablate one key frequency and see how much accuracy falls.
- What to notice: patching turns "this component is active during the behavior" into "this component is needed for the behavior".
- Build: precomputed; large.

**Check.** Patching one head from the clean run into the corrupted run restores 60% of the logit difference. What does that show? Answer: that head carries much of the information that decides the answer. It does not show that the head computes the answer by itself.
