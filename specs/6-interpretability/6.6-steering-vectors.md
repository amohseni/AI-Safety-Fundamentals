## 6.6 Steering vectors

**Aim.** The reader can build a steering vector (a direction added to a model's activations) and see it change behavior, and understands steering both as a causal test of a representation and as a way to control a model.

**Text (draft).**

> If a concept is a direction, adding that direction to the residual stream should make the model behave as if the concept were more present. Turner et al. (2023) took the difference between the activations for the prompts "Love" and "Hate", scaled it, and added it at one layer while the model wrote text; the text became more positive. Contrastive activation addition (Rimsky et al. 2024) averages such differences over many pairs of prompts that differ in one behavior, such as sycophantic and non-sycophantic answers.
>
> Steering serves two purposes. It tests a representation causally: if adding a "truth" direction changes what the model asserts, the model uses that direction. It also controls behavior without retraining. In 2024 Anthropic released Golden Gate Claude, a version of Claude with an SAE feature for the Golden Gate Bridge turned up; it brought the bridge into nearly every answer and in some contexts said it was the bridge. Arditi et al. (2024) found that removing a single direction disables refusal in many open chat models. That finding shows both the power of the method and how fragile safety training can be when it rests on one direction.

**Image.** Three continuations of "I went to the park and" with the coefficient on a positive-sentiment direction set to −5, 0, and +5.

**Interactive: "Steer the model".**

- Display: the continuation; a plot of a behavior score (for example, sentiment) and of fluency (how probable the text is under the unsteered model) against the steering coefficient.
- Controls: steering vector (sentiment, formality, a topic such as weddings, refusal for a small chat model); layer; coefficient from −10 to +10; a builder for the reader's own vector from two contrasting prompts.
- Try: (1) Raise the coefficient until the text stops being fluent. (2) Apply the same vector at an early, a middle, and a late layer. (3) Build a vector from two prompts of your own.
- What to notice: there is a range where behavior changes and fluency holds; past it, the text falls apart.
- Build: live with GPT-2 small in the browser if feasible, otherwise precomputed; large.

**Check.** Adding the "truth" direction at layer 10 does not change the model's outputs. What can you conclude? Answer: some evidence that the model does not use that direction at that layer for its outputs, or that the intervention was wrong in size or place; the probe's success alone never showed use.
