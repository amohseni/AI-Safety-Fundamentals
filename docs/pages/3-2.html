Embeddings

A language model receives a sequence of token ids (Section 3.1). Its first step is a lookup table. The model has a matrix with one row for each token in the vocabulary, the token embedding matrix, and each token id selects one row. That row, a list of numbers, is the token’s embedding. In GPT-2 small each embedding has 768 numbers, so the matrix has 50,257 rows and 768 columns.

The numbers in the matrix are parameters. They start as small random numbers and are adjusted by gradient descent along with every other parameter of the model (Section 2.6), so that the model’s predictions of the next token improve. Nothing tells the model which words are related. Whatever structure the embeddings have comes from training.

One way to see that structure is to measure how close two embeddings are. This page uses the cosine similarity: the dot product of two vectors divided by the product of their lengths. It is 1 for vectors that point the same way, 0 for perpendicular vectors, and negative for vectors that point in roughly opposite directions.

The tool uses word vectors from word2vec, a small model trained only to produce such vectors: the vectors are adjusted so that the vector of a word helps predict which words appear near it in text.Mikolov, Sutskever, Chen, Corrado, and Dean (2013), “Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality,” NeurIPS. The tool uses the vectors released with the word2vec software, trained on about 100 billion words of Google News text, 300 numbers per word. The page keeps 4,039 of them: the 4,000 most frequent words written in plain letters, and 39 more that the tasks and the figure use. A transformer’s embeddings are trained as part of the whole model, not on their own, but they show the same kind of structure (see the end of the page). In word2vec’s best-known example, the vector for “king” minus the vector for “man” plus the vector for “woman” lands near the vector for “queen”.Mikolov, Yih, and Zweig (2013), “Linguistic Regularities in Continuous Space Word Representations,” NAACL. The tool lets you check this.

Neighbors and analogies

Each dot is one of the 4,039 words, placed by a projection of its 300 numbers onto the plane. Search for a word to see its ten nearest neighbors by cosine similarity, computed from all 300 numbers. The analogy box computes \(a - b + c\) from the unit-length vectors and lists the five words closest to the result. The searched word and the analogy’s answers are in blue; the analogy’s three input words are in orange.

  1. Search for bank. Will its ten nearest neighbors include words about rivers?

    Search for bank. Then search for river.

    All ten neighbors are about money: banks, banking, Bank, branch, lending, deposit, mortgage, loans, accounts, loan. Word2vec gives each word one vector, learned from all the places the word occurs, and the vector for bank sits among the financial words. The neighbors of river (River, water, sea, bridge, boat, Lake) do not include bank. A transformer starts the same way, with one embedding per token, and uses its later layers to make the vector at each position depend on the surrounding words (Sections 3.4 to 3.6).

  2. Switch the exclusion off and compute king − man + woman. Which word will be closest to the result?

    Choose the king example, set Leave out a, b, c to Off, and compute. Then switch it on.

    With the exclusion off, the closest word is king itself, with cosine similarity 0.799; queen is second, at 0.712. Subtracting man and adding woman moves the vector only part of the way, and it stays closest to where it started. With the exclusion on, king cannot be an answer, and queen comes first.

  3. With the exclusion on, try three country-to-capital analogies: Tokyo − Japan + France, Paris − France + Italy, and Oslo − Norway + Austria. How many give the right capital as the first answer?

    The three examples are in the menu. Then switch the exclusion off and run them again.

    Two of the three. Tokyo − Japan + France gives Paris, and Oslo − Norway + Austria gives Vienna. Paris − France + Italy gives Milan first, with Rome a close second (0.721 against 0.704). With the exclusion off, only Paris survives: the Oslo analogy returns Austria, one of its own inputs.

  4. Search for king and switch the projection between PCA and UMAP. Which picture keeps more of king’s ten nearest neighbors among the ten dots nearest to it?

    Watch the readout under the map, which counts them. Try a few other words too.

    UMAP keeps 4 of king’s ten nearest neighbors among the ten dots nearest to it; PCA keeps none. Over a sample of 577 of the stored words, UMAP keeps 3.3 of the ten on average and PCA 0.3. Neither map keeps all ten: a picture in the plane loses most of what 300 numbers can hold.

What the analogies show

Six countries and their capitals, projected onto the two directions along which these twelve word2vec vectors vary most (their first two principal components). Each arrow goes from a country to its capital. The directions of the six arrows differ by at most 13 degrees, so in this picture “capital of” behaves like a direction. The projection is computed in your browser from the same vectors the tool uses.

Empirical finding Word vectors place words used in similar contexts close together, and some relations between words, such as country to capital or male to female, appear as roughly parallel differences between vectors. This is an early example of the linear representation hypothesis: the hypothesis that the features a model represents correspond to directions in its vector space (Section 6.2).

The analogy results are weaker than they are often presented. The standard analogy test leaves the three input words out of the possible answers. Empirical finding Nissim, van Noord, and van der Goot measured the same vectors on the standard test set of 19,544 analogies: with the inputs left out, the first answer was right for 74% of them; with the inputs allowed, for 21%. In most failures the answer was one of the inputs.Nissim, van Noord, and van der Goot (2020), “Fair is Better than Sensational: Man is to Doctor as Woman is to Doctor,” Computational Linguistics 46(2): 487–497. The 74% and 21% are for the rule this page uses, the one Mikolov et al. used. The analogies show a real tendency of the vectors, with a lot of noise around it.

A map in the plane cannot keep all the distances between vectors with 300 numbers. PCA keeps the two directions of largest spread and drops the rest. UMAP moves the dots so that each word’s nearest neighbors stay near it, at the cost of distances between distant words, which on a UMAP map mean little.McInnes, Healy, and Melville (2018), “UMAP: Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection for Dimension Reduction,” arXiv:1802.03426. The map was computed in advance with the umap-learn library (cosine distance, 15 neighbors, seed 0). The list of neighbors, computed from all 300 numbers, is what to trust; the map is only a picture.

Empirical finding GPT-2 small’s own token embeddings show the same pattern. The tokens nearest to “·bank” are “·banks”, “·Bank”, and “·banking”. Check

Take GPT-2 small before training, with its parameters at their random starting values. What would the nearest neighbors of the token “·bank” be?

The embeddings are parameters, set to random numbers before training. Only training on text moves the vectors of tokens that occur in similar contexts close together, so before training the nearest neighbors are whatever tokens happen to be close at random.

Word2vec reads the sentence “we sat on the bank of the river.” Which vector does it use for bank?

Word2vec has one vector per word, whatever the sentence. The first layer of a transformer works the same way: the embedding of a token is one row of a matrix. Only the later layers make the vector at each position depend on the context.

Takeaway

Each token becomes a vector, its embedding, by a lookup in a matrix of parameters learned in training. Words used in similar contexts get similar vectors, and some relations between words show up as directions, an early example of the linear representation hypothesis. The famous analogies are a noisy tendency: king − man + woman is closest to king itself unless the input words are left out of the answers.
