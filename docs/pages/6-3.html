Linear probes

A probe is a classifier trained to predict some property of the input, such as a word’s part of speech or the truth of a statement, from a model’s activations at one layer.Alain and Bengio (2016), “Understanding intermediate layers using linear classifier probes,” arXiv:1610.01644. A linear probe is a logistic regression: it computes a dot product of the activation vector \(h\) with a direction \(w\), adds a constant \(b\), and turns the result into a probability,

$$P(\text{property} \mid h) = \sigma(w \cdot h + b), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}.$$

The parameters \(w\) and \(b\) are trained on labeled examples to minimize the log loss of page 2.5, plus a penalty on the size of \(w\) (regularization). If the probe is accurate on examples it was not trained on, the property can be read out linearly at that layer. A two-layer probe puts a hidden layer of 64 ReLU units before the output, so it can draw curved boundaries.

A probe’s accuracy has to be compared with something. Hewitt and Liang proposed control tasks: give each word type a random label, the same label every time the word occurs, and train the same kind of probe to predict those labels.Hewitt and Liang (2019), “Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks,” EMNLP 2019, arXiv:1909.03368. The random labels mean nothing, so a probe can learn them only by memorizing which word it is looking at. The selectivity of a probe is its test accuracy on the real property minus its test accuracy on the control task.

The tool uses the model of page 6.2, Qwen2.5-0.5B, at every second layer from 0 (the embedding) to 24, and three properties. Truth: the 440 city statements of page 6.2, read at the final period, half for training; the control task gives each city a random label. Noun and inside quotation marks: word tokens from 34 passages of Pride and Prejudice, training on half the passages and testing on the others, with as many examples of each label as of the other.Austen (1813), Project Gutenberg eBook 1342, public domain. Nouns were tagged automatically by spaCy’s small English model (en_core_web_sm 3.8.0), so a few labels are wrong. A token counts as inside quotation marks if it lies between an opening and a closing curly quotation mark. Only tokens that begin a word are used. As on page 6.2, each activation is reduced to its coordinates along the 32 principal directions of the activations at that layer, and probes are trained in your browser on those coordinates.

What accuracy shows

Argument High probe accuracy shows that the property can be read out at that layer. It does not show that the model uses it. A probe can succeed by picking up information that the model carries as a by-product of other computations, and a flexible probe can learn part of the task itself, as the two-layer probe’s control accuracy shows. Selectivity guards against the second problem. The first needs a causal test: remove or change the direction the probe found and see whether the model’s behavior changes, as in the ablation above and in activation patching (page 6.7).Belinkov (2022), “Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances,” Computational Linguistics 48: 207–219, reviews these problems.

Probes are also used to monitor models. A probe trained to detect a dangerous property, such as a model writing an answer it represents as false, can run on every output at little cost. For such uses the key question is whether the probe still works on cases unlike its training data. Page 7.2 tests a truth probe on negations, paraphrases, and other topics.

Check

A probe scores 95% on the real task and 93% on its control task. What does that suggest?

The probe nearly fits random labels that carry no information about the property, so its accuracy reflects what it can memorize. Selectivity is 2 points: weak evidence that the representation encodes the property in a readable form.

A linear probe reads a property at 97% with high selectivity. What further test would show that the model uses the property?

Accuracy and selectivity are both facts about what can be read out. Use is a causal claim, so it needs an intervention on the activations and a change in behavior.

Takeaway

A linear probe is a logistic regression on a model’s activations at one layer; its test accuracy shows that a property can be read out there. Accuracy alone overstates what a probe shows. A control task measures how much the probe can learn by itself, and selectivity is the difference. Whether the model uses the property is a separate, causal question, tested by removing or changing the probe’s direction and watching the model’s behavior.
