Start here

This site explains how modern AI systems work, in enough depth to follow current AI safety research and to start contributing to it. It assumes linear algebra, calculus, and probability, and no machine learning.

After this page the site has nine sections. Section 1 is a short history. Sections 2 and 3 explain what a neural network is and how a transformer, the architecture behind current language models, computes its output. Section 4 explains how today’s models are trained. Section 5 asks what these systems can do in principle and collects results that surprised the field. Sections 6 and 7 cover how researchers try to find out what a trained network has learned, and when it is correct to say a system believes or wants something. Section 8 explains why a trained system can pursue goals its designers did not intend. Section 9 lists open problems and ways to start on them.

Every page answers one question with one interactive tool. Read the short text, make the prediction each task asks for, use the tool to check it, and then read what to notice. Each page ends with a check question and a box for explaining the idea in your own words. Your answers stay in this browser. Twenty pages, marked with a dot, form a core path of about five hours.

Four objects recur, so later pages build on objects you already know: a small network that labels points in the plane; a small transformer that learns to add numbers modulo 59, which Section 6 opens up to find its algorithm; GPT-2 small, a real language model, used wherever one is needed; and a gridworld, a small grid in which an agent moves, used in Sections 7 and 8.

Takeaway

The site goes from what a network computes (Sections 2 and 3), through how models are trained and what they can do (Sections 4 and 5), to how we find out what they have learned and want (Sections 6 and 7), why they can pursue unintended goals (Section 8), and where to start contributing (Section 9). Every page asks for a prediction before it shows the answer, and four running examples carry ideas from page to page.
