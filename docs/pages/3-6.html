The residual stream and the MLP

Each position in a transformer carries a vector, called the residual stream. In GPT-2 small it has 768 numbers. The embedding writes its first value: the vector for the token plus a vector for its position. Each layer then reads the current vector, computes something, and adds the result to it. No layer replaces the vector; each adds to what is already there. At the end, the final vector is turned into a score for every token in the vocabulary, and softmax turns the scores into the probabilities of the next token.

A transformer layer, also called a block, has two parts. The attention part (Sections 3.4 and 3.5) moves information from earlier positions into the current one. The MLP part, a small two-layer network applied to each position separately, takes the 768 numbers to 3,072, applies an activation function (GELU, a smooth version of ReLU), and takes the result back to 768. Each part reads the residual stream through a layer norm, which subtracts the mean of the vector’s entries, divides by their standard deviation, and applies a learned scale and shift; each part then adds its output back to the stream. GPT-2 small has 12 such layers, numbered 0 to 11.The name and the picture of the residual stream as a shared channel that every layer reads and writes come from Elhage et al. (2021), “A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits,” Transformer Circuits Thread.

Three of GPT-2 small’s 12 layers. The vertical line is one position’s residual stream. Each attention part and each MLP part reads the stream through a layer norm and adds its output back at a plus sign. At the bottom, a last layer norm and the unembedding turn the final vector into a score for each token.

Because the final vector is read by a fixed last step, a layer norm followed by the unembedding, the same step can be applied to the residual stream after any layer. This gives the token the model would predict if the computation stopped there. The method is called the logit lens.nostalgebraist (2020), “interpreting GPT: the logit lens,” LessWrong. The earlier layers were not trained to be read this way, so the lens is a rough view. It works better on GPT-2 than on some later models: Belrose, Ostrovsky, McKinney, Furman, Smith, Halawi, Biderman, and Steinhardt (2023), “Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens,” arXiv:2303.08112, found it unreliable on BLOOM, GPT-Neo, and OPT 125M. It is a way of looking at the residual stream; it does not change what the model computes.

    “Rome” first becomes the top prediction after layer 8, with probability 0.72, and stays on top to the output. After layers 0 to 4 the lens predicts “the” (with probability 0.63 to 0.84 after layers 0 to 3, and 0.27 after layer 4); after layers 5 to 7 it predicts word fragments (“La”, “Ing”) with probability under 0.2. The city appears only from layer 8 on. The embedding row predicts nonsense (“destro”): the lens reads the embedding poorly.

  2. Tap the cell where “Rome” first appears. Which part of that layer raised the probability of “Rome” more?

    Read the change made by each part, under the top 10.

    The MLP. Before layer 8, “Rome” has probability 0.04; after layer 8’s attention, 0.17; after its MLP, 0.72. The changes are measured one after the other, attention first, as the layer computes them.

  3. Now choose “The capital of Japan is the city of”. In the layer where “Tokyo” first becomes the top prediction, will the MLP again make the larger change?

    Find where “Tokyo” first appears in the last column, and tap it.

    Attention this time. “Tokyo” first becomes the top prediction after layer 9. Before layer 9 it has probability 0.07; layer 9’s attention raises it to 0.76, and its MLP to 0.86. The logit lens shows where in the stack a prediction forms; which part forms it differs from prompt to prompt, and the lens alone does not show how.

  4. Back on the Colosseum prompt. From after layer 9 to the output, after layer 11, what happens to the probability of “Rome”?

    Tap the last three cells of the last column. Then check the other prompts.

    It falls from 0.86 after layer 9 to 0.45 after layer 10, and to 0.02 at the output, where “Rome” is still the top prediction, just ahead of “P” (0.018). In all six prompts, the last layer lowers the probability of the top token while keeping it on top: it spreads probability over more tokens. A possible mechanism is described below the tasks.

What the layers add

Empirical finding The MLP layers can be read as key-value memories.Geva, Schuster, Berant, and Levy (2021), “Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories,” EMNLP 2021. Each of the 3,072 hidden units has a row of the first matrix, which acts as a key: the unit’s activation is large when the residual stream points along that row. Each unit also has a column of the second matrix, which acts as a value: the MLP adds that column to the residual stream, scaled by the unit’s activation. Geva et al. found keys that respond to patterns in the input text and values that raise the probability of tokens likely to come next after such patterns. Empirical finding Many factual associations, such as that the Colosseum is in Rome, appear to depend largely on MLP layers in the middle of the model, at the last token of the subject (“um” in “Colosseum”).Meng, Bau, Andonian, and Belinkov (2022), “Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT,” NeurIPS 2022. They found these MLP layers by patching (Section 6.7) and edited single facts by changing one MLP matrix. How localized that storage is remains debated: the layer where patching finds a fact is often not the best layer at which to edit it.Hase, Bansal, Kim, and Ghandeharioun (2023), “Does Localization Inform Editing? Surprising Differences in Causality-Based Localization vs. Knowledge Editing in Language Models,” NeurIPS 2023. The tool on this page reads only what each layer adds at a position, so it cannot show where a fact is stored. In the Japan prompt, the attention in layer 9 can raise “Tokyo” at the last position by bringing in information from earlier positions, such as “Japan”.

Empirical finding In GPT-2 small, some neurons in the last layer lower the model’s confidence without changing which token it ranks first: they enlarge the residual stream in directions the unembedding hardly reads, so the final layer norm shrinks every score.Stolfo, Wu, Gurnee, Belinkov, Song, Sachan, and Nanda (2024), “Confidence Regulation Neurons in Language Models,” NeurIPS 2024. They call these entropy neurons. Such neurons may be one cause of the fall in task 4; this page did not measure their contribution. Argument The loss is a proper scoring rule (Section 2.5), so it penalizes an output that is confident and wrong. A last layer that lowers confidence where the model often errs reduces that penalty.

Because every layer reads from and writes to the same vector, the residual stream is the natural place to look for what a model represents. Section 6 looks there.

Check

Can layer 8 use information computed by layer 2?

Layer 2’s output was added to the residual stream, and layer 8 reads the residual stream, which still contains that addition. Later layers add to it but do not replace it, so nothing has to pass layer 2’s result along. They can, though, add something that cancels part of it.

In the Japan prompt, layer 9’s attention part raises “Tokyo” at the last position from 0.07 to 0.76. What can the attention part do there that the MLP part cannot?

The MLP acts on each position separately, so at the last position it can use only what is already in that position’s residual stream. Attention takes a weighted average of values computed from the residual streams at the positions it may see, itself and earlier ones. Neither part changes the earlier positions: in a language model, information moves only forward.

Takeaway

Each position carries one vector, the residual stream, which the embedding starts and every layer reads from and adds to. Attention moves information between positions; the MLP transforms it at each position, and acts in part as a key-value memory. Reading the stream after each layer with the logit lens shows a prediction being built up gradually, by additions to one vector.
