The timeline

Most of the ideas behind today’s AI are old. The perceptron, a single artificial neuron that learns, dates from 1958. Backpropagation, the method used to train networks with many layers, was worked out by 1970 and became widely known in 1986. Convolutional networks read handwritten digits in 1989. None of these dominated AI until after 2012, when graphics processors (GPUs) and large labeled datasets made it practical to train large networks.

Before then, funding and attention rose and fell twice. The two collapses are called the AI winters, roughly 1974 to 1980 and 1987 to 1993. Each followed a period in which public claims ran well ahead of what the systems could do.Nilsson (2009), The Quest for Artificial Intelligence: A History of Ideas and Achievements, Cambridge University Press. The dates of the winters vary by source; some accounts extend the second to 2000.

The timeline below has three lanes: ideas, computing power and data, and the funding climate. Many events come with a well-known story. Where the story differs from what the documents show, the event’s card shows both: the lore and the record. Every event links to its source.

Takeaway

Most core ideas of modern AI are decades older than their success. Backpropagation, convolutional networks, and the LSTM were in place by 1997; what changed after 2010 was computing power and data, and the training compute of AI models began to grow much faster than before.
